# Delta Air Lines: gate power without aircraft fuel combustion

Evidence checked 12 September 2026. Suggested record: `delta.json`; original downloaded pages and retrieval manifest: `delta-sources/`. No application files changed.

## Strongest usable result

**For a cohort matching the original gate-electrification study, 1,000 baseline lifecycle tonnes corresponds to 630–970 tonnes avoided.** The suggested app uses 630 tonnes, the low study endpoint, explicitly as a normalized benchmark. It is **not** a measured Delta fleet opportunity, guaranteed minimum, or 63% of airline emissions.

Greer, Rakas and Horvath (2021), *Environmental Research Letters* 16, 054039, report the gate-electrification benchmark. The author’s dissertation reproduces the study with methods and Appendix B. **The denominator is partly electrified current practice, not pure fossil operation.** Table 7 (printed p.45) defines scenario 2b as 70% turnaround using 400Hz electric ground power plus diesel PCA and 30% APU. Scenario 3a uses 400Hz power and electric PCA throughout. Section 3.3.1 and Figure 10 (p.53) explicitly compare 3a against 2b. Scenario 1, 100% APU, is a different baseline.

Manufacturing and operational energy lifecycle impacts are included; end-of-life is excluded (Table 8, p.45). The normalized baseline must therefore include the existing electric component and match equivalent power/cooling service, aircraft and operating conditions. Already electrified gates can have a residual opportunity from actual use patterns; installed equipment alone does not establish no remaining potential.

The reported average 1–2-year payback uses the **100% APU baseline**, not the emissions comparison’s partly electrified baseline (§3.2.2, p.50). It cannot be paired with the 63–97% environmental result as though both used the same counterfactual. [Original-author methods and Appendix B](https://escholarship.org/content/qt08w701t9/qt08w701t9.pdf); [journal record](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/abf7f1); [institutional abstract](https://its.berkeley.edu/publications/reduce-aviation%E2%80%99s-greenhouse-gas-emissions-through-immediately-feasible-and-affordable).

The journal download remains blocked, but dissertation methods and the scenario table were verified through the web PDF reader. Detailed Delta project reconstruction remains necessary.

## Inventory, period and organizational boundary

The latest located annual inventory reports **39,301,990 tCO₂e Scope 1 in calendar 2025**. Mainline jet fuel contributes 37,134,782 tonnes; wholly owned airline subsidiaries 2,013,382; the remaining lines concern SAF nonbiogenic GHG and ground operations. Biogenic SAF combustion CO₂ is separately disclosed at 203,346 tonnes. [Delta GHG inventory, 2025 column](https://esghub.delta.com/content/esg/en/2025/ghg-emissions-data.html).

The boundary covers airline operations under Delta’s operational control, including Endeavor, Delta Vacations, Delta Material Services and Delta Flight Products. **Monroe Energy’s refinery is excluded**, except relevant fuel supply-chain emissions in Scope 3. Non-wholly-owned Delta Connection carriers belong in Scope 3. [Boundary, Metrics and Targets](https://esghub.delta.com/content/esg/en/2025/climate-related-disclosures.html). Reporting dates are January 1–December 31, 2025; selected first-quarter 2026 developments are separately noted. [About this report](https://esghub.delta.com/content/esg/en/2025/about-this-report.html).

At the pilot’s fixed $255.12/tCO₂e valuation in 2024 dollars, the reported Scope 1 inventory represents approximately **$10.03 billion modeled societal climate damage**. This is neither cashflow nor liability, and excludes aviation contrails and other non-CO₂ warming effects, purchased energy, much of the value chain, local pollution, noise, water and biodiversity. The model is not a total planetary damage measure.

## Delta relevance and the remaining opportunity

Delta reports 59 million gallons of operational savings during 2025 against a no-improvement counterfactual; those savings already occurred. Its first Atlanta maintenance pad was electrified in 2025, with projected savings up to 7 million gallons annually: an existing investment, not unfinanced remaining potential. Fifteen additional PCA units were installed; Atlanta gate telematics coverage reached 81% and indicator-light coverage 54%. These percentages do not measure gates lacking electric power. [Delta 2025 progress, Efficient Aircraft Operations](https://esghub.delta.com/content/esg/en/2025/advancing-progress.html).

Delta also published a useful marginal sensitivity in April 2025: reducing systemwide APU runtime by one minute corresponds to 1.2 million gallons annually. At EPA’s 9.75 kgCO₂/gallon jet-fuel factor, this is **11,700 tonnes of gross direct CO₂** before replacement energy and lifecycle impacts. It does not show how many extra minutes can safely be removed or precisely define every operating cohort. Never combine it mechanically with the study’s lifecycle percentage. [Delta APU sensitivity](https://news.delta.com/media/image/64251); [EPA factors, Table 2](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2021-04/documents/emission-factors_mar2020.pdf).

Commercial deployment is established by actual installed infrastructure and FAA financing precedent. An April 2025 FAA guidance letter makes gate-electrification projects available for consideration at all commercial-service airports; its specific update supersedes older attainment-area restrictions still appearing on the general VALE page. [FAA program guidance](https://www.faa.gov/airports/aip/guidance_letters/R-PGL-25-02-AIP-Discretionary-Set-Aside).

The deployable cohort still requires gate-level APU logs, installed-power/PCA availability and utilization, aircraft service requirements, weather, turnaround durations, marginal electricity use and grid capacity. Preserve power reliability, thermal comfort and safety. Avoided fuel costs must cover electricity, maintenance, installation and financing; airport-airline contracts must allocate those savings. No capital-cost quote, current project payback or positive fund-additionality bound has been established.

## SAF: useful context, no unsupported 80% scenario

Delta’s 2025 SAF procurement was 23.4 million neat gallons, including physical fuel and certificates. The report says SAF was below 1% of fuel use; supply, price premiums and uncertain offtake delivery constrain growth. Physical-only volume was not identified. [Cleaner Fuel and footnote 5](https://esghub.delta.com/content/esg/en/2025/advancing-progress.html). Compared with 4.269 billion gallons for mainline plus Connection, procurement equals about **0.55%** of that fuel pool; this is a scale comparison, not a verified physical blending share. [Fuel table](https://esghub.delta.com/content/esg/en/2025/fuel-efficiency.html).

No generic SAF lifecycle reduction is applied. A future SAF calculation needs feedstock, conversion pathway, hydrogen/electricity source, land-use treatment, certificate ownership and a matched fossil lifecycle comparator. Neat SAF volume and physical aircraft combustion cannot be conflated, and biogenic CO₂ is not absent from the exhaust.

## Alternative screened and excluded from funding claim

Delta announced installation of VCT finlets across 240 Boeing 737-800/900ER aircraft in June 2026 following operational evaluation. This supports commercial availability but is already committed, and the source does not publish a fuel-reduction percentage. No invented untreated-fleet count or percentage enters this record. [Delta finlet announcement](https://news.delta.com/delta-advances-fleet-efficiency-vct-finlets-across-737ng-fleet).

## Valuation and index qualification

The normalized lifecycle benchmark values at **$160,725.60–$247,466.40** per 1,000 matching baseline tonnes, using the existing pilot factor. This screening valuation is separate from the fuel-cost cashflows that might repay capital. Do not add normalized cases to company totals or count airport and airline savings twice.
