# Nucor: feasible interventions and limits on investable savings

Evidence cutoff: 12 September 2026. Research, not an investment commitment. Nucor already uses electric arc furnaces; replacing nonexistent Nucor blast furnaces is excluded.

## Main finding

Commercial furnace improvements, cleaner electricity and better scrap are applicable. Nucor also has a contracted Louisiana DRI carbon-capture project. Its own near-term roadmap quantifies a **116 kg CO2e/t hot-rolled steel reduction**, but this is a company plan against **2023**, not measured remaining abatement as of 2026 and not investor additionality. The latest 2025 actual is **1,051 kg/t**, leaving **76 kg/t (7.23%)** to the 2030 target, not the full historical 116 kg/t. Public data do not establish an incremental, principal-repaying project portfolio.

**S1. The roadmap is 1,091→975 kg CO2e per metric tonne hot-rolled steel, 2023→2030. Components: capture ~35 (Scope 3), reheat optimization ~26 (Scope 1), electricity ~25 (Scope 2), charge/injection carbon ~15 (Scopes 1+3), pig-iron improvements plus low-copper shred ~15 (Scope 3). Scope totals change 207/282/602→170/257/548. The company discusses supersonic injection, reheat efficiency, low-copper shred and lower-carbon pig iron. Sebree’s 250 MW solar VPPA began generation September 2025; 100 MW Western Trail wind began 2021. Kingman’s 50 MW battery operated late 2025; 25 MW solar is planned for construction 2026. Sedalia has a 75 MW wind arrangement. These are already operating or executed commitments. RECs support Econiq products; they do not themselves establish physical avoided emissions.

**S2. Tenova, 18 September 2017, Nucor Seattle commissioning results.** [Original supplier report](https://tenova.com/newsroom/press-releases/tenovas-nextgenr-i-eafr-and-water-detection-technologies-significantly). April 2016 controls commissioning was followed by reported average monthly consumption reductions: electricity 5%, oxygen 1.5%, natural gas 11.5%, injected carbon 7.5%, charge carbon 32%; combined electrical/chemical energy cost per ton fell more than 6%. This is supplier-reported plant evidence, not an independently randomized test. No absolute baseline kWh/t, capital cost, current fleet adoption or current tariff is disclosed.

**S3. Tallman Technologies and Nucor Seattle, “Decarbonization at Nucor Seattle using Tallman Supersonic Carbon Injectors,” pp.4–9, Figures 6–10, Table 2.** [Original engineering paper](https://tallmantechnologies.ca/pdf/Decarbonization%20at%20Nucor%20Seattle%20Using%20Tallman%20Supersonic%20Carbon%20Injectors%20%28TSCi%29.pdf). Two injectors installed March 2021. January 2019–February 2021 baseline total carbon feed 25.35 lb/cast ton fell to 13.60, a reported 46.3% reduction; high-energy-program electricity fell 3.3%. Feed comparisons cover 2021 operation, with a production-program change complicating causal attribution. The third injector lacked compressed-air capacity. The authors found broadly similar melt-in carbon and slag FeO, and no increased sulfur pickup, but requested further data. The paper reports 18,800 tons CO2/year; ton convention and oxidation accounting are insufficiently explicit for merging this with corporate metric-tonne inventory. Use material/energy factors rather than that annual claim. This Seattle installation is already in the baseline, not an unexploited project.

**S4. US DOE Better Plants, Charter Steel Saukville scrap-preheat case.** [Original government-hosted partner case](https://betterbuildingssolutioncenter.energy.gov/showcase-projects/charter-steel-sustainable-scrap-preheat-system). Reported 17% energy savings; 2.25 million kWh/year less electricity; 7,500 tons/year less scrap; nearly $4 million/year savings; approximately 14,000 tons/year less GHG. Partner-reported rather than DOE endorsement. Retrofit commissioning initially diverted about 25% of heats to other products because specifications varied. Its natural-gas quantity appears unit-ambiguous and is excluded. Do not transfer its combined financial/GHG result to Nucor or add it to controls savings. It demonstrates availability and material commissioning risk, not Nucor adoption.

**S5. Nucor advanced material recovery case, 16 May 2025.** [Company case study](https://nucor.com/2025/05/16/advanced-material-recovery-case-study/). Bushnell, Florida uses adapted mineral-processing equipment to recover nonferrous metals. Nucor claims doubled copper/nonferrous recovery volume. This has a different material stream and outcome from copper concentration reduction in steel scrap. No public recovered tonnage, processing energy, marginal virgin-iron replacement or cost is provided; do not treat “2x” as a steel GHG factor.

**S6. Nucor/ExxonMobil capture agreement, 1 June 2023; ExxonMobil update 26 January 2026.** [Nucor agreement](https://investors.nucor.com/news/news-details/2023/Nucor-Enters-Into-Carbon-Capture-Storage-Agreement-with-ExxonMobil-06-01-2023/default.aspx), [2026 project-status update](https://corporate.exxonmobil.com/news/viewpoints/2025-taking-carbon-capture-and-storage-from-momentum-to-impact). Convent, Louisiana DRI has a contracted capture/transport/storage ceiling of 800,000 metric tonnes CO2/year. The later update still expects 2026 startup; these sources do not prove achieved annual capture. Capture is not net lifecycle avoidance: energy penalty, transport/storage emissions and upstream gas emissions require accounting. Exclude its already contracted capacity from a new fund’s additionality.

**S7. EPA AVERT guidance, Questions 5–6.** [Original methodology](https://www.epa.gov/avert/avert-questions-and-answers). Physical electricity benefits need marginal rather than average grid rates. EPA describes AVERT as short-run and recommends limiting assessment to five years from the underlying regional data year; old regional factors should not be carried blindly into 2031. Long-run grid-change analysis requires suitable forward scenarios.

## Fixed-output computable scenario

**Purpose:** a bounded, reproducible intensity scenario, not a claim about enterprise tonnage or fund-enabled savings. Hold a representative block of **1,000,000 metric tonnes/year hot-rolled steel at the 2025 product mix and GSCC boundary** fixed through 2031. The block is an analyst-selected normalization, not a measured mill or assumed percentage of Nucor. Apply the company’s 2030 intensity target, assumed achieved and maintained in 2031; no speculative improvements beyond it.

- Latest baseline: 1,000,000 × 1.051 = **1,051,000 t CO2e/year** (2025 actual, S1 p.38).
- Target: 1,000,000 × 0.975 = **975,000 t CO2e/year**.
- Remaining target gap: **76,000 t CO2e/year, 7.23%** of baseline.
- Historical reference only: the 2023 baseline would produce 1,091,000 t, with a 116,000 t total roadmap reduction (10.63%). Its historic components are 41,000 furnace, 25,000 electricity, 15,000 iron/scrap, 35,000 capture. **Those historic component amounts cannot be assigned to the remaining 76,000 t gap:** progress by measure is undisclosed.

This is a **target-achievement scenario**, not an independently validated engineering forecast. The roadmap includes existing commitments, can include market-based electricity accounting, and does not establish the still-unimplemented fraction by technology. Therefore **remaining uncommitted 2026 technical gap: unknown; fund-additional avoided emissions: unknown; bankable principal-repaying capital: unknown**. Zero is assigned to the fund’s *credited* savings until additionality is demonstrated, not to physical technical potential. No cumulative five-year reduction is calculated because a commissioning schedule is absent.

Do not multiply these factors by SASB cast-steel output, enterprise product shipments, or a mill intensity with a different boundary. A matching hot-rolled denominator and plant-level implementation inventory are required for absolute corporate potential. Nucor’s narrower mill Scope 1+2 or worldsteel comparison figures are not interchangeable with the GSCC roadmap.

A second check is independent of corporate production: S2’s 5% observed electricity reduction corresponds to **50 MWh saved per 1,000 MWh of qualifying baseline EAF electricity**, if replicated. Monetary savings equal 50 × the marginal avoided electricity price per MWh, minus incremental operating cost. Do not apply it to all enterprise electricity, or claim Seattle can repeat an already achieved reduction. S3’s 25.35−13.60 gives **11.75 lb less carbon material per source-defined cast ton**; converting that directly into CO2 requires carbon composition and oxidation/storage mass balances.

## Investment implication for a principal-preserving fund

Prioritize diligence on a named, still-unoptimized furnace with performance-based repayment from verified utility and consumable savings. A no-profit target still requires covering installation, servicing, monitoring, downtime and defaults. The evidence supports a project type, not any specific loan amount or allocating the entire billion-dollar fund to Nucor. Nucor’s ability to finance itself is also an additionality question: cheaper money alone does not prove acceleration. Separate project cash from estimates of social climate/health damages; avoided damages are not a repayment stream.

Missing before underwriting: named eligible assets and existing technology; matching production and grade mix; metered baseline electricity/gas/carbon; delivered input prices and demand tariffs; project bids, installation schedule and incremental maintenance; scrap substitution mass balances and supplier LCAs; hourly regional power profiles and forward marginal factors; PPA/REC ownership; measured capture energy and storage accounts; who receives incentives or product premiums; enforceable repayment contract; proof the fund changes the counterfactual.

Research limitations: an older company raw-materials presentation returned 404 and its search-snippet copper figure was excluded. Historical supplier claims are reported with their attribution and confounding limits. No proprietary plant eligibility or contracts were available. No external messages or transactions occurred.
