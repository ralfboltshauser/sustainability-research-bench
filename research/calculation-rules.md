# Five-company sustainability gap pilot: calculation rules

These rules define a proposed research and calculation design. They are not a recognized sustainability standard, nor evidence that every company can be scored from public information.

## Purpose and scope

The research asks which environmental improvements are supported by publicly available evidence for Microsoft, Walmart, ExxonMobil, JPMorgan Chase, and Nucor. The investment question is which additional improvements outside capital could enable while recovering principal. These are separate questions.

Information cutoff: September 12, 2026. Each company uses its latest verified quantitative reporting period, explicitly labeled; periods are not silently presented as simultaneous. Candidate solutions must be commercially available by the cutoff. A 2031 deployment horizon is a scenario convention, not a claim of adoption.

## Three separate outputs

1. Reported baseline observations: quantities, boundaries, fiscal or calendar periods, units, sources, and assurance status.
2. Intervention estimates: physically comparable baseline and solution scenarios, assumptions, required inputs, and possible reductions.
3. Financing hypotheses: use of capital, repayment source, additionality, risks, costs, and unresolved commercial diligence.

Financial exposure, ownership attribution, and reported inventory reductions do not by themselves prove causal physical improvement. An environmental valuation is a modeled societal cost, not company expenditure or investor revenue.

## Calculation levels

An intervention may reach one of these levels. They are states of calculability, not confidence grades or corporate performance grades.

| State | Meaning | Permitted result |
|---|---|---|
| Quantified reported outcome | A source reports an implemented project's outcome | Historical project result with exact attribution and caveats; not new funding impact |
| Company-bounded scenario | Matching company activity data and applicable intervention factors exist | An explicit conditional estimate with uptake left as a parameter unless evidenced |
| Unit-level scenario | A substantiated effect exists per physical unit but eligible company scale is unknown | Effect per unit and exact missing scale input |
| Research lead | Applicability, effect, or boundary cannot yet be established | Unquantified opportunity, not zero improvement |

## Core calculations

For a physical activity with matched service output and a common lifecycle boundary:

`annual reduction = eligible annual activity × adopted share × (baseline impact per unit − solution impact per unit)`

Adopted share is never supplied by an agent's unsupported judgment. A sensitivity such as per 1% adoption is an arithmetic illustration, not an adoption forecast. Embodied one-time savings and recurring annual savings remain distinct. A 2031 annual run-rate is not multiplied by five to imply a five-year cumulative benefit.

For a matched assessed boundary:

`gap percentage = 100 × modeled preventable harm / modeled baseline harm`

The numerator and denominator require identical activities, time period, valuation choices, and environmental dimensions. A carbon-only result is a climate gap, not an all-sustainability score. Estimates covering only selected interventions are not a complete company gap. Water withdrawal, water consumption, replenishment, recycling and avoided withdrawal are different measures.

Historical improvement, present technical opportunity and incremental investor impact are separate. The funding counterfactual includes existing commitments, expected replacement, and policy-driven improvements. Evidence of a company target is not evidence of a remaining attainable gap or investor additionality.

## Aggregation and comparison

Preserve Scope 1, Scope 2 location-based, Scope 2 market-based, Scope 3 categories, and financing attribution separately. Never add both Scope 2 methods. A bank's financed emissions may overlap the borrower and its customers. Physical-source aggregation is needed before claiming a total opportunity across companies.

An electricity-procurement contract can change inventory accounting without establishing the location and timing of physical system reductions. Use a project-specific physical counterfactual for avoided emissions claims. Displacing an activity to another company is not eliminating its damage.

Interventions addressing the same emissions source are not independently additive. Model a joint scenario or keep alternatives separate. Preserve harms shifted to other dimensions; avoid crediting electricity-related water or carbon benefits twice through separate impact pathways.

Absolute reported footprints can be displayed side by side with their boundaries. An ordinal ranking of achievable gaps is withheld if comparable numerator and denominator estimates do not exist. Low evidence coverage is not low harm. Missing significant dimensions make an overall environmental grade unavailable.

## Valuation policy

Physical results remain primary for this pilot. Monetary environmental valuations require a selected, versioned method, applicable geographic factors and emission years, and harmonized currency basis. No generic monetary factor is applied merely to produce a headline number. Different valuation scenarios must remain explicit. Interim methodologies are labeled as interim.

## Source and quality controls

Each numerical observation requires an exact primary source URL, document page or table when available, unit, reporting period, organizational/geographic boundary, and reported/derived/model designation. Company assurance does not automatically extend to future intervention estimates. Vendor estimates remain vendor estimates. Sector evidence can supply a conditional factor but cannot establish company uptake or eligible volume.

Review prioritizes boundary consistency and decision-changing arithmetic. Reconcile source totals, distinguish short tons from metric tonnes, and test whether percentage denominators match. Retain contradictory disclosures with an explanation rather than silently resolving them.

## Pitch limits

The defensible product promise is a traceable path from measured corporate activity to a bounded improvement opportunity. Findings from five companies do not establish coverage for the full index, the globally best use of a billion, or financial principal guarantees. A useful result can be an identified missing input that prevents a misleading investment claim.
