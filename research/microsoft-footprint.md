## Accounting and measurement cautions

The primary GHG baseline is **21.121 million tonnes CO2e** from GHGP Table 1A. The **20.290 million tonnes** management-criteria total in Table 1B is a different accounting presentation. Its alternative categories involve sustainable fuel certificates, aviation treatment and renewable electricity associated with sold-product use. Crucially, management Category 11 covers annual electricity of the active Surface/Xbox installed base, while GHGP Category 11 covers lifetime use of devices sold during FY25 (p16). Third-party devices running Microsoft software are excluded. The 831,000-tonne difference is not a new physical abatement opportunity and does not compare identical service cohorts. Scope 2 location-based and market-based inventories are alternatives, never additive.

Scope 3 categories sum to 18,243,000 tonnes. Adding Scope 1 and market-based Scope 2 gives 21,121,315 tonnes, correctly rounded to 21,121,000. Energy sums to 37,461,476 MWh. Water balances at 13,266 − 5,096 = 8,170 ML (8.170 million m3); waste diversion plus disposal equals 58,868 tonnes. HFC emissions and individual energy/waste streams are parts of their respective totals.

FY25 Scope 2 accounting excludes spot unbundled EACs after the procurement-policy change. Matched renewable electricity and the reported 100% direct renewable target use different definitions (Table 5 versus 6); neither establishes hourly physical zero-carbon supply. Historic values were restated for acquisitions and methodology. Most water discharge is estimated; office assumptions and datacenter cooling models appear on p17. Operational waste excludes construction/deconstruction.

Owned-site Table 15 offers a useful physical entry point but omits leased datacenters, commissioning activity, and small owned locations collectively below 1% of owned-fleet electricity/water. Its water/electricity ratio is **not WUE**: the numerator is withdrawal and denominator total site electricity, not cooling consumption per IT electricity. Replenishment is a separate intervention, not an automatic offset for local use.

WRI describes Aqueduct baseline conditions as modeled historical expectations, not real-time conditions. High-stress consumption is an exposure screen, not a damage valuation. [WRI FAQ](https://www.wri.org/aqueduct/faq)

## Inputs for fixed-service reductions by 2031

**Cooling efficiency / low-water retrofit** (Direct control; maintain FY25 service and reliability). Available: Regional electricity, Owned-site electricity/withdrawal pairs, Global stress-class consumption. Required: site IT load and useful service, cooling-only withdrawals and consumption, retrofit eligibility, climate-specific energy/water tradeoffs. Overlap: Energy saving changes Scope2 and Scope3 fuel/energy; same avoided electricity cannot also be credited to renewable procurement.

**Physical clean electricity supply and electricity efficiency** (Direct operations / electricity procurement). Available: 37,026,353 MWh electricity, Scope2 LB and MB separate, Regional electricity. Required: hourly geography and dispatch, additional delivery capacity by 2031, existing measures already in FY25. Overlap: Reduce demand first, then apply alternative clean-supply factor only to remaining load. Do not count both Scope2 accounting alternatives.

**Lower embodied hardware and construction emissions** (Supply-chain influence). Available: 9,044,000 tCO2e capital goods, supplier-data share 78%. Required: server versus building share, tonnes concrete/steel and number/chip type servers, replacement required to maintain baseline service, component LCAs and supplier retrofit feasibility. Overlap: Capital expenditure in growth year is not the replacement capital needed for fixed 2031 service; no blanket percent reduction to entire category.

**Equipment refrigerant management** (Direct control). Available: 70,131 tCO2e HFCs. Required: refrigerant equipment inventory, leak versus disposal losses, compatible lower-GWP alternatives. Overlap: Cooling retrofit and refrigerant replacement can affect same emissions and energy.

**Product energy and circularity** (Products; separate from operational assets). Available: 1,540,000 tCO2e GHGP sold-product use, operational waste streams. Required: product sales cohorts and service/lifetimes, energy telemetry, recovery destinations. Overlap: Product use inventory is lifetime cohort estimate; do not compare directly with annual cloud electricity or subtract renewable certificates twice.

## Missing data

- Public fleet service denominator: delivered compute, storage, inference/training and network service volume by hardware and site, rather than revenue.
- Site IT energy, PUE, cooling process consumption/withdrawal, hourly utilization and weather, temperatures and retrofit compatibility; owned-versus-leased split.
- Supplier/site product volumes and bill of materials; disaggregate capital goods into servers, semiconductors, buildings/concrete/steel; embodied emissions, replacement ages and useful lives.
- Hourly geographic energy demand/generation matching, marginal emission factors and grid/connection constraints, existing contracted supply and retirement plans.
- Basin and monthly water consumption, actual municipal source mix, discharge quality/temperature and ecological response factors; primary-meter coverage and uncertainty intervals.
- Waste construction/deconstruction mass, composition, treatment destinations and actual displacement from reuse/recycling.
- Species of refrigerants, equipment charge/leak rates and service/retrofit constraints; diesel generator duty hours and standby reliability constraints.
- Physical sold-product units by model, lifetime, use patterns and regional grids to separate real use-efficiency improvement from downstream certificate accounting.

No overall score or dollar harm estimate is produced. Data availability is not environmental performance. Carbon, water and waste require separate impact pathways; financial savings must not substitute for damage valuation.
