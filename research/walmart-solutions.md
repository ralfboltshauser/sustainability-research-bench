# Walmart — intervention evidence, September 12, 2026

Four commercially available intervention families are supportable. Public evidence does **not** yet support a credible company-wide 2031 adoption volume, net emissions reduction, capital allocation, or principal repayment forecast. The accompanying JSON records numerical factors, boundaries, dependencies, and explicit nulls. Normalized examples below are computable engineering modules, not estimates of Walmart's remaining opportunity.

## Baseline and attribution

Use FY2026 operational activity at constant service output; preserve the separate CY2025 food-waste period. Onsite refrigerants are 4.08 million tonnes CO2e annually. Walmart already had 619 U.S. facilities partially or fully using ultra-low-GWP refrigerants at December 31, 2025. Food sent to landfill/incineration was 1,420 million pounds in CY2025, covering global operational food reporting excluding India. These are different boundaries and must not be combined by site count. [Walmart FY2026 report, pp. 8, 31, 137](https://corporate.walmart.com/content/dam/corporate/documents/esgreport/2026/FY2026-Walmart-ESG-Report.pdf)

Technical gap means feasible improvement over fixed current activity. Investor additionality means the change caused by this financing beyond funded replacements, required compliance, and planned deployments. Neither a technology demonstration nor a corporate aspiration establishes additionality. Project Gigaton's project accounting must not be subtracted from Walmart's annual emissions inventory.

## 1. Refrigerant leakage and equipment replacement — strongest material candidate

Commission system-level leak detection and preventive maintenance; replace eligible high-GWP systems with commercial lower-impact equipment where lifecycle performance is verified. EPA documents individual supermarkets sustaining leakage below 5% annually with advanced systems plus operating practices. This historical US result is **not** a forecast for Walmart or an isolated maintenance treatment effect. [EPA guidance, printed p. 2](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/documents/leakpreventionrepairguidelines.pdf)

A bounded, measurable unit: preventing **1 kg/year of R-404A leakage avoids 3.922 tCO2e/year** of direct emissions using the EPA table's 100-year GWP of 3,922. CO2 refrigerant has GWP 1. Equal emitted mass replacement therefore gives a direct difference of 3.921 tCO2e/kg, but equal leakage mass is only a sensitivity: different equipment changes charge, leakage, and electricity consumption. This excludes manufacture, servicing recovery losses, and energy; harmonize GWP versions before adding to Walmart inventory. [EPA GWP reference](https://www.epa.gov/hfcs/technology-transitions-gwp-reference-table)

For actual systems, calculate charge × annual leakage fraction × GWP before and after at identical cooling service, then include measured electricity change and recovery losses. Do not sum a full leak-repair reduction and a full refrigerant-replacement reduction for the same emissions.

A maintenance shared-savings contract could repay capital through avoided gas purchases and repairs. Replacement financing needs actual net operating savings or contracted lease/service payments. Missing: gas-specific charge and leak logs, cooling duty, asset ages, already-approved replacement schedules, equipment bids, technician capacity, and incremental energy costs. No 2031 adoption share is invented.

## 2. Residual building-lighting efficiency — demonstrable, remaining scale unknown

DOE/PNNL's Walmart Leavenworth parking-lot comparison reports 44% lighting electricity savings against 400-W pulse-start metal halide and 63% against a 1,000-W comparator. At the historical $0.056/kWh tariff, respective simple paybacks were 6.2 and 7.5 years. This was a 2012 new-site design comparison, not a current retrofit quote. [DOE/PNNL case study, p. 2](https://www1.eere.energy.gov/buildings/commercial/pdfs/walmart_leavenworth_case_study.pdf)

**Normalized conditional scenario:** a fixed 100 MWh/year of eligible comparator lighting yields 44 or 63 MWh/year saved; at the source tariff, energy bills fall by $2,464 or $3,528 annually. The two endpoints are different equipment comparators, not uncertainty limits. This excludes maintenance in the cash calculation and does not imply any eligible Walmart sites remain.

Audit the remaining fixture stock before underwriting an equipment lease or energy-performance contract. This opportunity could already be largely exhausted. Measure current bills, quotations, operating hours, illumination requirements and maintenance savings. Use only saved electricity and a consistent site emissions factor; efficiency and renewable procurement cannot each claim the original same electricity emissions.

## 3. Electric yard trucks — targeted, commercially demonstrated

Walmart reports electric yard equipment delivering over 75% lower emissions per operating hour than diesel. Its disclosure does not establish trial geography, electricity accounting, or vehicle lifecycle boundary. Treat that percentage as a corporate operational claim needing validation; never apply it to total fleet emissions or long-haul trucking. [Walmart FY2026 report, p. 33](https://corporate.walmart.com/content/dam/corporate/documents/esgreport/2026/FY2026-Walmart-ESG-Report.pdf)

Hold yard tasks and operating hours fixed; compare actual diesel use against electric consumption with equal payload, uptime and service. Add charging demand charges and separate battery/vehicle manufacturing impacts. A truck-and-charger lease can be repayable if measured fuel and maintenance savings cover payments. Missing: remaining diesel yard fleet and hours, raw performance data, truck and charger prices, grid connections, tariffs, and already-ordered replacements. There is no supported company-wide reduction or 2031 conversion percentage.

## 4. Food prevention and recovery — separate food saved from disposal diverted

Walmart reports de-packaging at more than 4,500 U.S. locations, with 60% more organics recovered and 12% less compactor trash. These are recovery/trash outcomes, not prevention or carbon-reduction factors; the raw trial design is not public. Do not multiply these percentages by global food disposal. [Walmart waste disclosure](https://corporate.walmart.com/purpose/esgreport/waste-and-circularity)

Forecasting, markdowns and cold-chain monitoring should target avoidable spoilage; de-packaging addresses the remaining recovery stream. Prevention requires evidence that procurement or production falls before crediting upstream avoidance. Diversion needs local treatment comparisons including methane capture, electricity, transport, and displaced products. Food prevented cannot also receive full diversion credit. Composting and digestion remain food loss in the FLW accounting boundary even when preferable to disposal.

There is a plausible repayment source in reduced write-offs, purchasing, and disposal charges. The original WRI/WRAP study's grocery subgroup has **10 sites and a 5.1 benefit/cost ratio**, rather than the often-quoted all-sector median. Subgroup geography and project duration are not separately disclosed, and this selected historical sample is neither annual return nor a Walmart forecast. [Original study, pp. 6, 14–15](https://docs.wbcsd.org/2017/03/Report_The_Business_Case_for_Reducing_Food_Loss_and_Waste.pdf)

A process-improvement loan or shared-savings agreement should be based on actual net procurement, margin and waste-management savings. Food donations and monetized environmental benefits do not by themselves repay principal. Missing: commodity-level avoidable mass, food throughput, eligible sites, existing programs, incremental operating costs and local disposal fees/factors.

## Investment conclusion

Refrigeration merits the first asset-level diligence request because the reported direct footprint is material and avoided refrigerant spending is measurable. Yard electrification and food prevention are useful targeted candidates; residual lighting should pass an inventory screen before further diligence. No allocation from a billion-dollar fund is justified yet. Establish a signed list of eligible assets, the no-finance replacement schedule, vendor capacity through 2031, project quotations, and enforceable cashflow rights before estimating either repayable deployment or investor-attributable reductions.
