# Microsoft: deeper water and deployment evidence

As of 12 September 2026. The existing 15% climate / 31% lifecycle blue-water matched-study case remains intact. No defensible company-wide preventable-water percentage emerges.

## Baseline and boundaries

FY25 withdrawal was 13.266 million m³, consumption 8.170 million, discharge 5.096 million. High/extremely-high-stress consumption was 3.926 million m³ (48.1%). These are different physical quantities; stress is exposure, not quantified ecological damage or preventability. Most sites lack discharge meters. Phoenix's owned cluster reports 981,000 m³ withdrawal and 954,206 MWh site electricity; their ratio is not WUE because the electricity denominator includes infrastructure. Source: [factsheet](https://cdn-dynmedia-1.microsoft.com/is/content/microsoftcorp/microsoft/msc/documents/presentations/CSR/2026-Microsoft-Environmental-Data-Fact-Sheet-PDF.pdf), Table 8 p7, methodology p17, Table 15 pp25–26; site data are outside assurance.

## Stronger evidence of interventions already operating

Phoenix reports a 23% FY25 year-over-year WUE improvement from controls and operational changes. Fleet WUE was 0.27 L/kWh in 2025. A separate 25% achieved / 40% targeted improvement uses withdrawal per MW against 2022, not WUE. Microsoft also reports more than $500 million of water/wastewater investment across more than 75 projects since 2020. These establish action, not remaining opportunity or retrofit payback. [June 2026 operational update](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/06/24/inside-microsofts-two-decade-push-to-cut-water-intensity-while-scaling-for-growth/), smarter-controls section, opening footnote 1, investment section.

Wisconsin Fairwater was operating in April 2026, according to the [April earnings call](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/events/fy-2026/earnings-fy-2026-q3), opening infrastructure remarks. Its engineering description assigns over 90% of capacity to closed-loop cooling; remaining traditional servers can use water during hot conditions. This is the Fairwater context, not a global 90% zero-water fleet claim. [Engineering description](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2025/09/18/inside-the-worlds-most-powerful-ai-datacenter/), environmental-impact section. Actual deployment therefore extends beyond a purely future-pilot description.

## Projected savings and tradeoffs

The December 2024 announcement projects more than 125,000 m³ annually per datacenter, based on withdrawal WUE of 0.30 L/kWh. It is not an independently measured consumption saving. The same announcement says mechanical cooling raises PUE, partly mitigated by warmer coolant and economizing; it gives no numeric electrical penalty. It scheduled Phoenix/Wisconsin pilots in 2026 and broader new-design operation from late 2027. No verified Phoenix pilot commissioning was found. [Design announcement](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-cloud/blog/2024/12/09/sustainable-by-design-next-generation-datacenters-consume-zero-water-for-cooling/), opening/footnote, energy and implementation sections.

Coolant recirculation alone does not prove dry heat rejection: follow heat to the outdoor rejection stage. Electricity-generation water and carbon must be included in site assessment. Reuse/nonpotable supply can reduce potable demand while water still evaporates. A 31% lifecycle blue-water saving cannot be multiplied by the onsite corporate inventory. [Nature study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08832-3), Table 4 p336 and Figure 4/p337; retained matched virtual-core assumptions.

A further real impact is cooling-fan noise: Microsoft's Mount Pleasant updates identify the equipment source and mitigation work; the July update continues evaluation. There is no quantified exposure/damage estimate. [Local engineering updates](https://local.microsoft.com/blog/testing-underway-to-understand-noise-at-our-mount-pleasant-datacenter/), April 15, June 18 and July 20 2026.
